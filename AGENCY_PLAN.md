# Xentenix: From Freelancer to Agency

## The core idea
You already have proof: about 10 shipped AI products, 4 blog posts, and past clients. Xentenix's job is to **repackage that proof under the agency name**, then go out and contact people with a **clear, narrow offer**. The website helps conversion, but the outreach is what brings in revenue. Run both from week 1, not one after the other.

---

## Phase 0: Positioning (decide before building anything, about 2 days)

1. **Pick a niche, at least for outreach.** "AI agency for everyone" doesn't convert. Choose 1–2 target customers, for example:
   - SaaS companies that want AI features (Onboarding Agent and Headline AI are proof)
   - EdTech and training businesses (ExamineAI, Career Compass, your trainer background)
   - SMBs that want ops automation or "AI FTEs" (matches the current Xentenix messaging)
2. **Define 3 productized offers** instead of "we build anything":
   - **AI Audit / Discovery Sprint**: low fixed price or free. This is the foot-in-the-door.
   - **AI Agent / MVP Build**: fixed scope, 4–6 weeks, fixed price.
   - **AI FTE retainer**: monthly, you run and improve the automations.
3. **Define the guarantee.** The hero says "Don't pay us a single penny if you don't get the desired result." That's a strong hook, but you need written terms: what counts as "the result," milestone payments, and what's out of scope. Otherwise it will hurt you.
4. **Be honest about how your past work is framed.** A founder-led agency can say "Built by the Xentenix team" or "Founder-led projects." Two warnings from the personal site:
   - `PortfolioClient.tsx` has about 20 generic entries ("AI-Driven Drug Discovery Platform", "Smart City Traffic Management"…) that look like placeholders. **Don't bring those over.**
   - The stats "$1M+ revenue / 20+ clients / 99.5% satisfaction" will be questioned on sales calls. Only use numbers you can back up.

## Phase 2: Business setup (in parallel, weeks 1–3)

- **Templates:** proposal, contract or SOW (scope, milestones, the guarantee terms, IP), and invoice.
- **Payments:** a business bank account or Payoneer/Wise. Register the company when revenue justifies it.
- **Profiles:** LinkedIn company page, Upwork **Agency** account (move your freelancer reviews over), Clutch, GoodFirms, Google Business.
- **CRM:** HubSpot free or a simple Notion pipeline (Lead → Contacted → Replied → Call → Proposal → Won/Lost).
- **Cold-email infrastructure: start this on day 1.** Buy 2–3 secondary domains (e.g., `getxentenix.com`) and warm them up for 2–3 weeks. Never cold email from your main domain.

---

## Phase 3: Outreach (starts week 2 and runs continuously)

**Channels, in order of how quickly they usually pay off:**
1. **Warm network:** past clients, LinkedIn connections, students from your training work. Announce Xentenix and ask for referrals. This is often the fastest first deal.
2. **Founder-led LinkedIn:** post 3–4 times a week (build stories, demos, lessons). Send 20–30 targeted connection requests a day to your niche.
3. **Cold email:** use Apollo for leads and Instantly/Smartlead for sending. Write short, personal emails that open with a specific observation about their business.
4. **Loom audits for high-value leads:** a 3-minute video showing *their* website or workflow and where AI could save them time. This converts very well.
5. **Upwork Agency:** keep it as a steady inbound source.

**Weekly targets for the first 90 days:**
- 150–200 new contacts reached → about 5–10% reply → 3–5 calls → 1 proposal
- Track these numbers in the CRM and adjust the message every 2 weeks based on reply rate.

**Content loop:** every project becomes a case study, a LinkedIn post, and a blog article. That feeds the website and outreach at the same time.

---

## Phase 4: Delivery and scaling (month 2 onward)

- Build a **bench of 2–3 trusted contractors** (frontend, backend, design) so you can sell more than you can build yourself.
- Write SOPs: onboarding checklist, weekly client update template, handover doc.
- Collect a testimonial and permission to publish a case study at the end of every project.
- Once you have 1–2 retainers, consider a first hire for sales or delivery.
